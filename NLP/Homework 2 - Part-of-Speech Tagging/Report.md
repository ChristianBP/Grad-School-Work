## HMM
For the HMM, I found it to be a natural extension to Homework 1 where we made a bigram model. The algorithms for calculating the emission and transition probabilities were almost identical to the bigram algorithm. However, with the use of the viterbi algorithm, we were able to consider more than just the previous tag or word. It allowed us to use probability of the full sentence in our predictions. We could find the probability that the sentence led to the previous tag, and then use that to weight the emission and transition probabilities. Putting all the probabilities together allowed us expand on the idea behind the bigram predictor and give a fairly accurate prediction for the POS tag of a word within a sentence, rather than just predicting the bigram. The HMM's biggest pitfall seems to come from inaccurate predictions when there is insufficient or imprecise training data. For instance, the word 'process' was marked as a noun when it should have been a verb. When you look at the data, it's understandable why this happened. Within the training set, 'process' showed up 195 times as a noun and only once as a verb. Therefore, the probability of a token being a verb and emitting 'process' was extremely low when compared with the probability of a token being a noun and emitting 'process'. In roughly the same fashion, all the errors that the HMM made could be traced back to the training data.

## RNN
For the RNN I learned about what parameters were best suited for this specific tagging model. I tried a few different activation functions but found that all of them except softmax were causing the loss to explode. I researched that ReLU is the go to activation function for deep learning. It is extremely fast compared to sigmoid and tanh, avoids gradient descent, and doesn't activate all the neurons at the same time. However, it didn't work for this model because ReLU only works as the activation function for a classification problem if there are more layers at the end of the model that don't use ReLU. I also learned that cross entropy loss is typically used for classification and in this instance I had to use categorical cross entropy loss because I was inputting the data in the form of one hot vectors. I tried a varying the learning rate and found that 0.013 was the most optimal of those that I tried. Both 0.1 and 0.001 were causing the loss to explode. 0.1 seemed to be learning too fast and 0.001 was too slow. Both 0.007 and 0.1 managed to make it to the end without the loss exploding and only had two inaccuracies. However, both were beat out by 0.013 which only got one tag wrong.
